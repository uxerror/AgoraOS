  Four months since #6. Two of us moved, one of us got a real job, and
  our server spent a week in a closet with no power. It lives. So do we.

  [1] bbs memories                                                  moth

  Before the web there was the board. One phone line, one caller at a
  time, and a sysop who knew everybody's handle. You waited for the line
  to be free, you read the new messages, you left one, you hung up so the
  next person could call. Downloads came at 2400 baud and you planned
  your evening around them.

  What I miss is the size of it. Forty regulars, a message base you could
  read in an hour, and a sysop who answered your questions himself. The
  net is bigger and faster. I am not sure it is friendlier.

  [2] the 486 that would not die                              halfduplex

  It came out of an office that was throwing it away: a 486DX2-66 with
  8 MB of RAM and a fan that sounded like a lawnmower. It has been a
  router, a print server, a mail box for the house, and now it plays MP3s
  in the kitchen. Uptime last week: 211 days. Lesson: never throw out a
  machine that still boots.
